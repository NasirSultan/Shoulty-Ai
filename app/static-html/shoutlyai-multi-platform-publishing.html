The multi-platform problem

Publishing manually to 10 platforms is a full-time job. It shouldn’t be.

Every platform has different image sizes, caption limits, hashtag rules, and posting interfaces. Publishing one piece of content across all of them takes 45–90 minutes of repetitive, error-prone work — every single post.

📷
Every platform needs a different image size — manually
Instagram wants a 1:1 square. Facebook wants 1.91:1 landscape. TikTok wants 9:16 portrait. Pinterest wants 2:3. YouTube thumbnails are 16:9. Resizing one image for all 10 platforms means opening Canva 10 times and exporting 10 files. Most businesses skip this and post misformatted content that looks unprofessional.
📄
Caption rules are completely different on every platform
Instagram captions need a hook in the first line and 30 hashtags at the end. LinkedIn posts need short paragraphs and 3 hashtags maximum. X has a 280-character hard limit. Google Business has a 1,500-character limit with no hashtags. Copying the same caption to all platforms violates at least 4 of these rules simultaneously.
🔁
Logging in and out of 10 different accounts daily
Open Instagram. Switch to business account. Upload image. Write caption. Add hashtags. Post. Open Facebook. Log into business page. Upload differently-sized image. Rewrite caption. Post. Repeat for LinkedIn, X, TikTok, YouTube, Google Business, Pinterest, Threads, Bluesky. That’s 90 minutes of tab-switching before you’ve done any real work today.
📌
Platforms are missed entirely because it’s too much work
Most businesses that manage their own social media end up active on 2–3 platforms and ignoring the rest. They know they should be on Google Business. They know Pinterest drives traffic. They know LinkedIn builds credibility. But logging into each one manually every day is simply not sustainable alongside running an actual business.
🚫
Third-party tools break connections without warning
Most generic multi-platform tools use unofficial API workarounds that Instagram and Facebook actively block. Every few weeks, you wake up to find your connections have silently failed — and 2 weeks of scheduled posts never published. You find out when a customer notices you’ve gone quiet, not from a notification.
🔍
No way to preview how posts look on each platform before publishing
What looks great on Instagram can look wrong on LinkedIn. The image crop that works on Facebook cuts off the subject on Pinterest. The caption that fits on Instagram gets truncated on X. Without platform-by-platform previews before publishing, formatting errors reach your audience before you can fix them.
