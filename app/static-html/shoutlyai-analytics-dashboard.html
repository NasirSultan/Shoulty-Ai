The analytics problem

Most businesses collect data from 10 platforms and use none of it.

Native platform analytics are siloed, inconsistent, and buried inside apps you have to log into separately. You end up making content decisions based on gut feel — while your data sits unused across 10 different dashboards.

🔁
Logging into 10 different apps just to understand your performance
Instagram Insights, Facebook Business Suite, LinkedIn Analytics, YouTube Studio, Google Business Insights, TikTok Analytics — each has a different interface, different metric definitions, different date ranges. Understanding your overall performance takes 45 minutes of tab-switching, so most businesses never actually do it.
📐
Every platform measures metrics differently — nothing is comparable
Instagram’s “reach” and Facebook’s “reach” are calculated completely differently. LinkedIn’s impressions include repeat views; Instagram’s don’t. Comparing performance across platforms using native analytics means comparing apples to mangoes. You can’t make decisions from data that doesn’t speak the same language.
📈
No way to see which content type is actually driving growth
You know Reels get more views than carousels. But by how much? Are the views converting to followers? Which caption style drives the most DMs? Native analytics show vanity metrics but never tell you which content decisions are actually moving the needle on business outcomes.
🕐
No data on the best time to post for your specific audience
Every article says “post at 9am on Tuesdays” — generic advice for generic audiences. Your restaurant’s followers in Koramangala peak at 7:30am and 8pm on weekdays. Generic time advice costs you 30–50% of your potential reach on every single post.
📄
Client reporting takes 4–6 hours to compile manually
For agencies, monthly reports mean opening each platform, screenshotting graphs, copying numbers into a presentation, formatting it, and writing commentary. 4–6 hours per client per month, multiplied by your full client roster. It’s the highest-cost, lowest-value activity in running a social media agency.
🚫
Data that’s weeks old by the time you act on it
Most businesses do a monthly analytics review — acting on 4-week-old data. The Reel that went viral last Tuesday should have been replicated the next day. The content type that tanked in week 2 should have been dropped in week 3. Delayed analytics means delayed decisions and slower growth.
